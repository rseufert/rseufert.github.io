mock-edi

A mock EDI trading partner. Not an EDI library or an AS2 server, but the thing on the other end. Send it an 850 and it answers with a 997, an 855, an 856 and an 810. Send it EDIFACT ORDERS and you get CONTRL, ORDRSP, DESADV and INVOIC.

   you ──850──▶  mock-edi
       ◀──997──  the syntax parsed
       ◀──855──  2 lines: one confirmed, one short
       ◀──856──  shipment / order / item, with a tracking number
       ◀──810──  1132.80, terms 2% 10 net 30
  • Zero dependencies. Installs in a locked-down CI image.
  • X12 and EDIFACT over AS2. Fixed-width ISA, release characters, HL trees, MDNs with a Received-Content-MIC.
  • It can be the buyer, not only the seller. POST /_mock/purchase places an order with a partner whose role is supplier - so if the system under test is the one that sells, the mock is the customer sending you the 850 and reading your 855, 856 and 810 back, saying where each disagrees with what it ordered. It also receives a remittance advice, the X12 820 and EDIFACT REMADV, and reports one that advises cash before it settles.
  • AS2, plain HTTP, or a folder. Point --drop-dir and --pickup-dir at directories for integrations that still trade files.
  • Changes and receipts. An 860 changes an order and is answered with an 865; EDIFACT's ORDCHG is answered by an ORDRSP, having no change acknowledgment of its own. The 997s you send back are matched to what the mock sent, so "nobody acknowledged my invoice" is testable. A replayed interchange is refused with a TA1, not fulfilled twice.
  • Failure on demand. Short shipments, rejected lines, duplicate invoices, documents out of order, answers an hour late, corrupt trailers, a translator that rejects everything, an invoice that never comes, a strict partner, a partner that never answers.
  • Validates its own output against the same dictionary it checks yours against.

mock-edi --port 8080
curl -X POST --data-binary @order.edi http://127.0.0.1:8080/edi
