mock-sap

A black-box mock SAP endpoint. It speaks the shapes, not the business logic: SAP Gateway OData V2 and V4 services, BAPI/RFC calls over JSON and SOAP, and IDocs in XML and flat-file form, all backed by SQLite.

  • Zero dependencies. Python 3.8+ standard library and SQLite.
  • Real wire shapes. __metadata, /Date(…)/, EDMX $metadata, CSRF tokens, $batch changesets, BAPIRET2 tables.
  • V2 and V4 over the same rows. $apply aggregation, delta tokens, Fiori UI annotations, and OAuth 2.0 token flows a client can expire and refresh.
  • Both sides of the document chain. A sales order becomes a delivery, an invoice and money owed to you; an inbound INVOIC becomes a supplier invoice and money owed by you - an open item a payment run selects with one $filter, and the supplier's IBAN to pay it into. Post the bank's FINSTA01 statement back and the invoices it paid are cleared; a returned payment reopens one, and every line the statement cannot place is reported rather than guessed at.
  • Posting is remembered, not just answered. A posted DELVRY, INVOIC or FINSTA01 keeps what it decided: which sales orders a delivery moved, the supplier invoice and accounting document an invoice created, which invoices a statement cleared and why it refused a line. Read the IDoc back and you get the same answer the POST gave, so a client that dropped the response can still find out; /_mock/idocs?settled= asks from the invoice's side, which is the question an integration actually has: what settled this one?
  • An invoice is not paid twice behind your back. An open item says which payment run has it in flight, as PaymentRunID and PaymentRunDate, so a second run started before the statement comes back can leave it alone. And a credit line on a FINSTA01 has to say what it is: RET for a payment of ours coming back, RCV for money arriving. A credit that says neither reverses nothing and is listed as unprocessed with the reason, because either guess is wrong somewhere: RET pays an invoice twice, and RCV hides a payment that came back.
  • A supplier can be told what was paid. Generate a REMADV from a payment document and you get a PEXR2002 naming every invoice that payment settled, with the number the supplier quotes and what came off it, and a total that is the sum of those rows. A statement that pays several invoices for one supplier posts one payment covering all of them, the way a payment run does, which is what makes the advice worth sending.
  • Deterministic demo data. Business partners, products, sales and purchase orders, the same every run for a given seed.
  • Failure on demand. Latency, 500s, locked documents, expired CSRF tokens - and the two that answer success and mean no: an inbound IDoc SAP accepts and then declines to post, and a BAPI that returns HTTP 200 with TYPE E in RETURN for a valid call. Both look like success from the sending side, which is why they are the ones worth testing against.
