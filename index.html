Projects

Mock enterprise systems for integration testing. Point your code at them and develop, test and demo without a license, a VPN tunnel, or a support ticket.

Using them together: Testing an SAP-to-EDI integration without SAP or a trading partner. On the failures you can't ask a partner to perform: three EDI behaviours you can't rehearse.

The films are drawn by mock-films from the messages of a real run against each mock. The mocks have no screens of their own; these are the conversations they had.

mock-sap

A black-box mock SAP endpoint. It speaks the shapes, not the business logic: SAP Gateway OData V2 and V4 services, BAPI/RFC calls over JSON and SOAP, and IDocs in XML and flat-file form, all backed by SQLite.

  • Zero dependencies. Python 3.8+ standard library and SQLite.
  • Real wire shapes. __metadata, /Date(…)/, EDMX $metadata, CSRF tokens, $batch changesets, BAPIRET2 tables.
  • V2 and V4 over the same rows. $apply aggregation, delta tokens, Fiori UI annotations, and OAuth 2.0 token flows a client can expire and refresh.
  • Both sides of the document chain. A sales order becomes a delivery, an invoice and money owed to you; an inbound INVOIC becomes a supplier invoice and money owed by you - an open item a payment run selects with one $filter, and the supplier's IBAN to pay it into. Post the bank's FINSTA01 statement back and the invoices it paid are cleared; a returned payment reopens one, and every line the statement cannot place is reported rather than guessed at.
  • Deterministic demo data. Business partners, products, sales and purchase orders, the same every run for a given seed.
  • Failure on demand. Latency, 500s, locked documents, expired CSRF tokens - and the two that answer success and mean no: an inbound IDoc SAP accepts and then declines to post, and a BAPI that returns HTTP 200 with TYPE E in RETURN for a valid call. Both look like success from the sending side, which is why they are the ones worth testing against.
pip install mock-sap
mock-sap --port 8000
curl "http://127.0.0.1:8000/sap/opu/odata/sap/API_SALES_ORDER_SRV/A_SalesOrder?\$top=1&\$format=json"

mock-edi

A mock EDI trading partner. Not an EDI library or an AS2 server, but the thing on the other end. Send it an 850 and it answers with a 997, an 855, an 856 and an 810. Send it EDIFACT ORDERS and you get CONTRL, ORDRSP, DESADV and INVOIC.

   you ──850──▶  mock-edi
       ◀──997──  the syntax parsed
       ◀──855──  2 lines: one confirmed, one short
       ◀──856──  shipment / order / item, with a tracking number
       ◀──810──  1132.80, terms 2% 10 net 30
  • Zero dependencies. Installs in a locked-down CI image.
  • X12 and EDIFACT over AS2. Fixed-width ISA, release characters, HL trees, MDNs with a Received-Content-MIC.
  • It can be the buyer, not only the seller. POST /_mock/purchase places an order with a partner whose role is supplier - so if the system under test is the one that sells, the mock is the customer sending you the 850 and reading your 855, 856 and 810 back, saying where each disagrees with what it ordered. It also receives a remittance advice, the X12 820 and EDIFACT REMADV, and reports one that advises cash before it settles.
  • AS2, plain HTTP, or a folder. Point --drop-dir and --pickup-dir at directories for integrations that still trade files.
  • Changes and receipts. An 860 changes an order and is answered with an 865; EDIFACT's ORDCHG is answered by an ORDRSP, having no change acknowledgment of its own. The 997s you send back are matched to what the mock sent, so "nobody acknowledged my invoice" is testable. A replayed interchange is refused with a TA1, not fulfilled twice.
  • Failure on demand. Short shipments, rejected lines, duplicate invoices, documents out of order, answers an hour late, corrupt trailers, a translator that rejects everything, an invoice that never comes, a strict partner, a partner that never answers.
  • Validates its own output against the same dictionary it checks yours against.
pip install mock-edi
mock-edi --port 8080
curl -X POST --data-binary @order.edi http://127.0.0.1:8080/edi

mock-bank

A mock bank that speaks ISO 20022 and NACHA, over HTTP and over a drop folder, so you can test a payment integration without a bank, a test IBAN, or six weeks of onboarding. It is not a ledger and not a payment provider: it holds four accounts, moves balances in minor units, and refuses by name anything it does not implement.

   you ──pain.001──▶  mock-bank
       ◀──pain.002──  2 accepted, 2 rejected (AC04, RC01)
       ◀──camt.054──  debited, on the settlement date
       ◀──camt.053──  the day's statement, and it reconciles
       ◀──pacs.004──  one came back, 3 business days later

Or the same four payments as an ACH file, for an account that banks in US formats. The choice is per account, so one file can produce both.

   you ──NACHA────▶  mock-bank
       ◀──text─────  ENTRY 999999990000002 INV-2026-0102 3400.50
                     REJECTED R02 the creditor account … is closed
       ◀──NACHA────  a return file: one 21 entry and addenda 99 per return
       ◀──BAI2─────  16,447,125000,Z,INV-2026-0101,…,Globex Supplies B.V./
                     16,447,1500000,Z,INV-2026-0104,…,Umbrella Logistics/
  • Zero dependencies. Python 3.8+, the standard library and SQLite, like its siblings. No transport needing cryptography, no screening, no direct debits - each refused by name, with an answer saying what is supported.
  • Two formats, and the mock does not care which door. A pain.001 or a 94-column NACHA file, over POST /payments or dropped in a folder, goes through the same decisions. An account whose format is nacha gets a plain-text acknowledgement where an ISO 20022 account gets a pain.002, an R-coded return file where it gets a pacs.004, and a BAI2 statement where it gets a camt.053.
  • Money can arrive, not just leave. POST /_mock/credits lands a customer's payment in an account on its value date, or the next business day for a weekend, a holiday or a missed cutoff. It is reported like everything else: a camt.054 as it books and an entry on the day's camt.053, which still reconciles - the payer as Dbtr, the note in RmtInf/Ustrd, and the invoice reference in Strd apart from it, because a bank that reformats remittance is the reason cash application is hard. A short payment, one that names no invoice, one that settles several, and a reference split across lines are each one request away.
  • The failures, not just the happy path. An account that rejects for AC04, one with no money, a bank identifier that is not the bank, a duplicate file, a statement with one payment missing, and a payment that settles and then comes back days later. The three behaviours the bank rejects for have NACHA twins: AM04 is R01, AC04 is R02, RC01 is R03.
  • Nothing sleeps. Settlement dates, the 15:00 cutoff, weekends and holidays run on a bank clock that POST /_mock/advance moves. A three-day return is a line in a test, not a three-day wait. And you need not wait for the day to end either: POST /_mock/accounts/<id>/report writes a camt.052 for the account as it stands now, which a reconciler can compare against the day's statement to find the entry a statement-gap leaves out.
  • Two doors, one pipeline. Most bank connections are two directories on an SFTP host rather than an endpoint, so a file dropped in --drop-dir goes through the same decisions as POST /payments and lands in processed/ or failed/ with the answer written beside it.
  • Its own output is checked against somebody else's files. Every ISO 20022 message is validated against schemas fetched from pinned commits, and the NACHA and BAI2 readers are held to files from moov-io - because a reader and a writer built from one declaration agree with each other even when both are wrong. That is how the mock found it was counting a returned debit as a credit, and that its BAI2 reader could not open a real bank's file.
  • Worked end to end with the other mocks. payment_run does what SAP's F110 does: select the open items that are due from mock-sap, pay them in one file, and post each statement back so SAP clears what was paid and reopens what came back. Twenty-three tests, run in CI against mock-sap from PyPI - twice, once over ISO 20022 and once over ACH, because the same run has to survive both.
pip install mock-bank
mock-bank --port 8090
curl -X POST --data-binary @pain001.xml http://127.0.0.1:8090/payments

Procure to pay all three mocks

One purchase, carried the whole way: a purchase order in SAP becomes an X12 850, the supplier confirms it, ships it and invoices it, a three-way match posts the invoice into SAP as money owed, a payment run pays it in an ISO 20022 pain.001, and the bank's statement clears it. Three of the four examples below are its individual legs; this is the one that runs end to end.

SAP  ──850──▶  supplier          a purchase order becomes an EDI order
     ◀──855/856/810──  supplier  confirmed, shipped, invoiced
SAP  ◀──INVOIC──                 matched, posted, and now owed
     ──pain.001──▶  bank         a payment run selects what is due
SAP  ◀──FINSTA01◀──camt.053──    the statement clears what was paid
  • It was written to find bugs, and it found three. Each pair of mocks was green while the chain was broken, because each pair's tests assert what the next system received rather than what it could do with it. An INVOIC that named no supplier and so created nothing owed; a mock that answered "posted" for having posted nothing; an order placed in EUR that came back invoiced in dollars. All three had been passing for days.
  • The same invoice twice, and two things that look like they catch it. A supplier retries an invoice after the first was taken, and a restarted middleware posts it again. Upstream, an invoice arriving alone is blocked for billing more than was shipped - because the restart forgot the ship notice too, not because anything checked. Downstream, the payment run skips a repeated reference within one run - and pays it in the next, once the first has cleared. Neither end is checking, and the second payment goes out through the gap.
  • The fix is to ask the system of record. Does SAP already hold a supplier invoice with this number, from this supplier? One $filter, and it survives a restart because SAP is where the answer lives. Per supplier, because an invoice number is only unique within one.
  • A price disagreement never reaches the bank, blocked by a real 810 disagreeing with the purchase order rather than by a flag a test set.
  • A payment the bank rejects leaves the invoice owed. The supplier's account is closed, SAP still holds its details, and AC04 comes back: the item stays open and stays distinguishable from one that was paid and came back.
  • It does not tell the supplier what was paid, and says so - which is why a supplier keeps dunning you for an invoice you paid. That needs a remittance advice, and as of mock-edi 0.6.0 there is one to send: it receives the X12 820 and EDIFACT REMADV. So the gap is now in the example rather than in the mocks, and the loop is closable.
pip install "mock-sap>=0.13.2" mock-edi
git clone https://github.com/rseufert/mock-bank && cd mock-bank
mock-sap --port 8000 &
mock-edi --port 8080 &
python3 -m mockbank --port 8090 --clock 2026-10-02T16:00 &
cd examples && python3 -m unittest -v test_procure_to_pay

PO bridge mock-sap + mock-edi

A worked example of the two mocks together. po_bridge.py reads a purchase order from SAP over OData, sends it to the supplier as an X12 850, and posts the supplier's 855 back into SAP as an ORDRSP IDoc. Any line that isn't a clean confirmation comes back as an exception for a buyer to look at.

send:     SAP PO (OData)  ──▶  X12 850  ──▶  supplier
receive:  SAP  ◀──  ORDRSP IDoc  ◀──  X12 855  ◀──  supplier
  • The supplier confirms everything, and one IDoc lands in SAP.
  • The supplier ships short, and both short lines are flagged.
  • The supplier refuses a line, and SAP's IDoc carries the rejection on the right item.
  • The supplier never answers, and nothing is posted: the case a chase-up alert is for.
  • SAP is down when the 855 arrives, and the confirmation is kept until SAP takes it. This test found a real bug.
git clone https://github.com/rseufert/mock-edi && cd mock-edi
pip install mock-sap
mock-sap --port 8000 &
python3 -m mockedi --port 8080 &
cd examples && python3 -m unittest -v test_po_bridge

Invoice check mock-sap + mock-edi

What happens after the goods ship. invoice_check.py is accounts-payable middleware: it takes a supplier's X12 810 invoice and posts it into SAP as an INVOIC IDoc only if it passes a three-way match against the purchase order and the 856 ship notice. Anything else is blocked, with the reasons.

PO (SAP)  ──┐
856 ship  ──┼──▶  match?  ──▶  yes: INVOIC IDoc into SAP
810 bill  ──┘                  no:  blocked, with reasons
  • A clean invoice is posted, and the test reads back the money owed - the supplier invoice, its line amounts and the open payable a payment run selects. Asserting only that SAP took the IDoc is what let this example post invoices that owed nobody anything, until mock-sap 0.13.2.
  • A short shipment, billed as shipped, is posted. Matching against the ordered quantity would wrongly block it.
  • A price disagreement is blocked. mock-edi bills its own catalogue price, the way real suppliers do.
  • An invoice in the wrong currency is blocked - and one that names no currency is not. The order declares its currency, so a EUR purchase order comes back invoiced in EUR; before mock-sap 0.13.3 it did not, and the match compared 12.50 with 12.50 without noticing they meant different money. Absence is not disagreement, so an invoice carrying no CUR segment still passes.
  • The same invoice sent twice is posted once. No sleeping: the test advances mock-edi's clock.
git clone https://github.com/rseufert/mock-sap && cd mock-sap
pip install mock-edi
python3 -m mocksap --port 8000 &
mock-edi --port 8080 &
cd examples && python3 -m unittest -v test_invoice_check

Pay invoices mock-edi + mock-bank

The step after the invoice is approved: paying it. pay_invoices.py takes a supplier's EDIFACT INVOICs, pays each on its due date in an ISO 20022 pain.001, and then works out from what the bank sends back which invoices are actually paid.

INVOIC  ──▶  pain.001  ──▶  bank
             pain.002  ◀──  accepted: scheduled, not paid
             camt.053  ◀──  on the statement: paid
  • Accepted is not paid. An invoice is paid when the statement shows the debit, not when the bank says it will try.
  • A retried run pays nothing twice. The same payments make the same MsgId, so the bank refuses the copy with DUPL, and that refusal does not undo the original.
  • A closed account, or no money, keeps the invoice open, held with the bank's reason code (AC04, AM04) rather than retried.
  • A returned payment reopens the invoice, and a payment missing from the statement is reported as unreconciled rather than cleared.
git clone https://github.com/rseufert/mock-bank && cd mock-bank
pip install mock-edi
mock-edi --port 8080 &
python3 -m mockbank --port 8090 &
cd examples && python3 -m unittest -v test_pay_invoices

Payment run mock-sap + mock-bank

The same step, but paying what is in SAP rather than what arrived over EDI. payment_run.py does what SAP's F110 does: select the open supplier items that are due, pay them in one pain.001, and post each camt.053 back as a FINSTA01 so SAP clears what was paid - and reopens what came back.

select:     SAP open items (OData)  ──▶  pain.001  ──▶  bank
reconcile:  SAP  ◀──  FINSTA01  ◀──  camt.053  ◀──  bank
            cleared · reopened · unreconciled
  • Accepted is not paid. A payment made after Friday's 15:00 cutoff settles on Monday. Friday's statement clears nothing, Monday's clears everything, and an integration that cleared on the acknowledgment reported cash it still had.
  • A returned payment and an invoice nobody paid are both open in SAP. One flag separates them: ClearingIsReversed. Without it you either pay the returned invoice twice or never chase it.
  • A statement that doesn't add up stops one payment, not the run. The arithmetic is checked before anything reaches SAP; the missing payment goes unreconciled and stays open. When two payments would equally explain the gap, both are named.
  • A blocked invoice is never selected, and the selection asks SAP to leave blocked and cleared items out rather than filtering them afterwards.
  • Anything either side did that the run couldn't use is said in words. run.problems is neither raised nor swallowed - a bank that answers with an error is three sentences, not silence. A host that is down raises and stops the run instead - a deliberate difference, not an oversight.
