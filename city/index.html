City

The site, from the street. Every part of it is a building you can walk up to and enter: the arena is a stadium with a live match on the scoreboard, the Julia set is the river, the dashboard is a weather station on a hill, the blog is a row of mailboxes, the backrooms are a manhole, the mocks are the towers, Dick Dank is on at the loft, and rickbot runs the helpdesk kiosk on the plaza. The streets are named in the elves' script, which drifts back into English while you stand still. The city is laid out from the date, so it rebuilds itself at midnight, and the sky is whatever time it is where you are.

Drawn the way the backrooms are drawn, out of doors: every wall is a four-sided figure filled black and outlined in light, painted from the far side of the city to the near, so a tower hides what is behind it; the walls that face you are cut into bays and floors when you are close, and some of the windows are lit after dark. The ground is a grid, the streets have curbs and centre lines, the river moves and is the Julia's colours, and the sky is a gradient for the hour, with stars, a sun or a moon where they should be. The city is seven blocks square. The landmarks take blocks by a shuffle seeded with today's date and the rest are filled with offices named from the site's lore; #seed=N in the address asks for another day's city and #hour=N for another sky. The scoreboard at the arena runs a real match in the arena's own engine, six tanks drawn as points on the board's plane, and starts another when it ends. One script, no library, plus the arena's engine for the scoreboard.
