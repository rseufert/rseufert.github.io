Flipper

Apps for the Flipper Zero: a 128×64 screen, a d-pad and a buzzer.

Hanzi Cards study

Mandarin flashcards: simplified or traditional characters, pinyin with tone marks, and the tones played on the speaker.

  • HSK 1 to 3. 150, 156 and 292 words, each deck with its own progress. HSK 1 and 2 come in a rough learning order; HSK 3 is shuffled.
  • Simplified or traditional, switchable at any time, with the characters drawn from Noto Sans CJK.
  • You hear the tones. When a card flips, the speaker glides through each syllable's tone contour - high and flat, rising, dipping, falling - so you hear the shape of the word.
  • Spaced repetition. Cards you miss come back often and cards you know come back rarely, and new cards only arrive while fewer than six are still being learned. Progress is saved to the SD card.

Step Seq music

A step sequencer for the Flipper's piezo speaker, with a melody grid and a drum row. The speaker can only play one tone at a time, so a drum takes the first few milliseconds of its step and the note gets the rest.

  • Always in key. One note per step across 15 pitch rows that follow the scale you pick: minor or major pentatonic, minor, major, blues or chromatic, in any root.
  • Drums from square waves and noise. A kick, a snare and a hi-hat on the bottom row.
  • Eight patterns, each 16 or 32 steps with its own tempo (60 to 240 bpm), swing, scale, root and octave. Copy one into the next slot to build a variation, and everything is saved to the SD card when you quit.

NW Crawl game

A tiny roguelike set on the alphabet streets of NW Portland. Start on Burnside and head north one street per floor - Couch, Davis, Everett, Flanders, all the way to Thurman - until you reach the Witch's Castle in Forest Park.

  • Nineteen streets and a castle. Each floor is a randomly generated maze of rooms, corridors and side alleys under fog of war.
  • The locals. Rats, crows, raccoons, rogue e-scooters and coyotes, getting tougher the further north you go. On the northern streets a Sasquatch lurks by the exit, invisible until he's two tiles away.
  • Coffee, donuts and IPAs. Coffee heals, a pink-box donut raises your max HP, a hazy IPA raises your attack and a used book shows you the floor. On Pettygrove, Joe's Cellar is a safe room where the bartender pours you one stiff drink.
  • The Witch hexes you from range, summons crows and vanishes when hit.

Yulia's Cats pet

A cozy virtual pet. Yulia shares a little room with her two cats: Nugget, an elderly tiger-striped gentleman with white socks, and Baby, a chonky grey cat with big bright eyes.

  • Feed, play and pet. Dinner for both, a treat each, yarn to chase, or the laser dot, which Baby cannot resist and Nugget cannot be bothered with. Pet one and the Flipper purrs.
  • The cats get on with their day. Nugget watches the birds from the window sill and keeps an eye on the food dish; Baby naps in the patch of afternoon sun, follows Nugget around and nudges him off his spot by the dish.
  • The room follows the clock. The sun crosses the window, the sky goes dark in the evening and the lamp comes on, and late at night the cats get sleepy on their own.
  • Yulia speaks Russian to the cats now and then, with the English underneath. She also reads, makes art and plays her keyboard through the speaker, and there's a wardrobe, decor, a record player in six genres and a photo album to fill.
  • Nothing bad ever happens. Time passes while the app is closed, so the cats get hungry and want to play, but that's all.

Tricorder tools

A handheld signal scanner, styled after a certain starship's field instrument. Eight sensors, and one sweep that runs them all.

  • RF. A Sub-GHz spectrum sweep with peak hold, or a scrolling waterfall. Lock on to the peak to chart its strength over time and name any protocol the firmware recognises. It only listens.
  • IR. The last infrared signal as a waveform, with its protocol, address and command. Send it back out, or save it to the SD card for the Flipper's own Infrared app.
  • NFC, RFID and iButton. Whether a reader field is nearby, which NFC protocols a tag answers to, and 125 kHz fobs, cards and iButton keys.
  • A probe and the battery. A GPIO pin's voltage (0 to 2.5 V) as a number and a trace, and the battery's voltage, temperature, charge and current draw.
  • It warbles when it has a contact, higher for stronger signals.
