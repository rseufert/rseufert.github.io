acme-treasury

A cash forecast you can check. It reads what mock-sap says is owed and what mock-bank says the account holds, and says what the account will close at on each of the next business days. Then the clocks are moved, mock-acme's payment run pays what is due each morning, and the bank's statements say whether the forecast was right. Most cash forecasts are never held to anything: by the time the day arrives, nobody looks back.

  • Held to the bank's statements. Both mocks keep a clock a test can move, so the tests play a week: a forecast every morning before the payment run, and every night the camt.053 for that day, which has to close where the forecast said, to the cent.
  • A payment at the bank is not owed twice. SAP keeps an item open until a statement clears it, so on the morning after a payment run the same invoice is an open item in SAP and a debit at the bank. They are joined on the EndToEndId and counted once.
  • Money that will not move is listed, not added up. A blocked payable is not money going out, an overdue receivable is not money coming in, and a payment the bank refused will be refused again. Each is listed with its reason. mock-sap seeds six overdue receivables, 684,875.06 EUR in all, and a forecast that believed them would be comfortable and wrong.
  • The calendar is the bank's. After the cutoff, today's run settles tomorrow, and nothing settles on a weekend or a holiday on the bank's list. A payment the bank will return comes back on its day, and is owed again.
  • It says where it will be wrong. A supplier closed their account last week and SAP does not know, so Monday's forecast expects to pay an invoice the bank then refuses. Monday is out by that invoice, and Tuesday's forecast lists it with the bank's reason. The film above is that week.
  • A payment schedule for when the line dips. plan is a small mixed-integer program, written in JuMP and solved by HiGHS: which invoices to hold, and until which business day, so that no day closes under a floor, holding as little as it can for as short a time as it can. plan --apply makes it happen as a user would, with a payment block in SAP on each held invoice, lifted on the morning it should be paid. It lifts only its own blocks.
